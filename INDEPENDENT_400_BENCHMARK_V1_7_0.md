# Independent 400-Ticker Benchmark — Cut-off EOD 6 August 2026

## Scope and limitation

The uploaded universe contains 400 active small/mid-cap Sharia candidates across eight IDX-IC sectors: 77 Consumer Non-Cyclicals, 69 Basic Materials, 60 Property & Real Estate, 49 Energy, 48 Industrials, 35 Infrastructure, 34 Technology, and 28 Healthcare.

This benchmark deliberately does **not** assign fabricated precise scores to 400 companies where current financial/news/flow evidence could not be independently verified in the build environment. All 400 names were used as the research universe and sector/investability funnel; the names below were promoted to deeper public-source review because they showed the strongest combination of earnings/future-fundamental evidence, balance-sheet/valuation quality, and identifiable catalyst. Final SMC/ICT entry must still be recomputed by the live v1.7 OHLCV scan using the same EOD cut-off.

## Asymmetric / narrative watchlist outside the risk-adjusted Top 10

**PKPK** remains one of the strongest asymmetric optionality names because Q1 2026 revenue/earnings inflected sharply and the DPAL acquisition can transform future earnings. It is *not* ranked near the top on a risk-adjusted basis because v1.6.4 already classified the price stage as `MARKUP`; the correct v1.7 state is more likely `WAIT_REACCUMULATION` unless fresh data show a new accepted base. **SSIA** also has a strong industrial-estate/Subang narrative and land-sales runway but should remain `WAIT_FUNDAMENTAL_CONVERSION` while return metrics/coverage and leverage are weaker than the leaders. **RAJA** has earnings resilience but Q1 revenue contraction and an advanced valuation/price location reduce the margin of safety. **RATU** had Q1 revenue and profit contraction and should not outrank stronger converters merely on narrative/flow.

## Why the v1.6.4 Top-3 was not robust

The old Top-3 was PKPK → MARK → JRPT. MARK and JRPT were credible discoveries, but the ordering was distorted by four implementation issues: generic growth was QoQ rather than same-quarter YoY; fundamental quality was underweighted; short-window flow could dominate lifecycle interpretation; and the Top-3 dashboard state ordering differed from the main engine. ELSA, WINS and ESSA demonstrate the opportunity cost: each had visible 2026 business/earnings conversion that deserved higher research priority than a low-evidence flow-only ranking.
